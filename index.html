📚 Carbon Footprint Knowledge Base

Carbon footprint refers to the total amount of greenhouse gas emissions directly or indirectly produced by an individual, family, enterprise or organization in daily production and life. It is an internationally recognized environmental quantitative indicator. All greenhouse gases are converted into carbon dioxide equivalent to directly reflect the environmental impact of the subject.

Carbon emissions in daily life are everywhere, covering energy consumption, transportation, diet, daily living and many other scenarios. Most people find it hard to perceive subtle carbon-emitting behaviors, but the accumulated daily consumption of a large number of individuals has a significant impact on the ecological environment. Understanding your carbon footprint and managing your personal emissions is the basic way for everyone to participate in ecological protection.

Core Tips for Low-Carbon Living

  • Save electricity: Most of China's electricity comes from fossil fuels, so every kilowatt-hour produces carbon emissions. Turn off lights when leaving, use energy-saving appliances and reduce standby power — the easiest way to go low-carbon.
  • Green travel: Private car fuel use is a major source of personal emissions. Prefer public transit, subway, cycling or walking to greatly cut travel emissions.
  • Low-carbon diet: Red meat breeding, processing and transport generate high emissions. Moderately reducing red meat, eating more plants and avoiding food waste can lower your dietary footprint.
  • Circular resources: Reduce single-use items, sort waste properly and reuse goods to cut indirect emissions from waste and incineration.

Why This Matters

Low-carbon development is not just a national policy, but a long-term cause for all. Small individual low-carbon actions can accumulate into a powerful force for ecological protection. This knowledge section aims to share professional yet easy-to-understand carbon footprint facts, remove blind spots in environmental awareness, and help everyone build green, low-carbon habits.
